# Ultrasound-Guided Injections of the Knee: Suprapatellar Approach (Handbook Page 68)

Intra-articular aspiration and corticosteroid injection of the knee joint are frequently performed to manage moderate-to-severe joint effusion secondary to osteoarthritis or inflammatory arthropathies. Landmark-guided palpation approaches exhibit variable failure rates, particularly in obese patients or those with dry, thickened synovial proliferation.

Under sonographic guidance, the patient is positioned supine with the knee maintained in fifteen to twenty degrees of flexion supported by a small bolster. A high-frequency linear array transducer is positioned sagittally or axially over the suprapatellar pouch superior to the patella. The quadriceps tendon, prefemoral fat pad, and suprapatellar bursa are clearly delineated.

Using an in-plane lateral approach, a 21-gauge needle is directed toward the maximal pocket of fluid. Direct acoustic observation ensures the needle tip lies freely within the synovial space without contacting articular cartilage or penetrating deep adipose tissue. Complete decompression of the effusion can be sonographically verified prior to injecting the therapeutic suspension.
