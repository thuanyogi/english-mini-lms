# Comparative Accuracy of Dynamic Ultrasound versus MRI in Partial-Thickness Rotator Cuff Tears

**Background:** Partial-thickness tears of the supraspinatus tendon represent a common source of chronic shoulder impingement and functional disability in middle-aged athletes. While magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) remains a reference standard, dynamic ultrasound offers distinct benefits in real-time kinetic evaluation.

**Methods:** We evaluated eighty-four consecutive patients presenting with painful arc syndrome who underwent both high-frequency dynamic ultrasound and 3.0-Tesla MRI prior to diagnostic arthroscopy. Diagnostic sensitivity, specificity, and positive predictive values were calculated using arthroscopic findings as the ground truth.

**Results:** High-resolution ultrasound demonstrated a sensitivity of 89.2% and specificity of 91.4% for detecting articular-sided partial tears, compared with 84.6% and 88.5% for conventional MRI. Dynamic abduction maneuvers during sonography significantly enhanced the detection of subtle delamination and bursal thickening.

**Conclusion:** Dynamic ultrasound is non-inferior to high-field MRI for identifying partial-thickness rotator cuff tears, with superior diagnostic yield when kinetic maneuvers elicit transient impingement under direct acoustic visualization.
